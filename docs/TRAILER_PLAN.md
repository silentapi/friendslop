# OVER BUDGET: announce trailer plan

**Target:** 54 seconds, 24 fps, 1280×720, with music and sound effects.
**Pitch it sells (the simple version, `SIMPLE.md`):** fix stuff = cash · wreck your friends' stuff = steal their cash · if the house isn't 100% when Grandma arrives, nobody gets paid.

## What the research says

- **Hook in the first ~5 seconds.** Steam autoplays the trailer, so don't open on a logo; open on the most striking moment. ([presskit.gg guide](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/game-trailers-screenshots-guide), [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-trailer-best-practices))
- **Structure:** hook (0–5s), then core loop (5–25s), then escalation (25–45s), then title and call to action (last 5–10s). 45–60s is the sweet spot for indies. ([itch.io guide](https://itch.io/blog/1030474/creating-a-trailer-that-sells-your-game-a-complete-guide-for-indie-and-aaa-developers.amp))
- **Gameplay first.** Show how it plays, not lore. ([wnhub](https://wnhub.io/news/analytics/item-237))
- **The friendslop recipe** (PEAK, R.E.P.O., Lethal Company, RV There Yet?):
  - voice-chat lines as subtitles, so the friends *are* the jokes;
  - ragdoll fails and slapstick;
  - short ALL-CAPS cards that state the rules;
  - an upbeat goofy track that drops out for the punchline;
  - "1–4 players · online co-op" and "Wishlist on Steam" at the end.

## Polish loop

For each scene:
1. Render 3–4 keyframes.
2. Check composition, readability, character poses and timing.
3. Fix, then re-render.

Repeat until clean. Then render the full sequence and review a contact sheet of the whole cut for flow (cut points, card timing, pacing), fix, and re-encode with the mixed audio.
