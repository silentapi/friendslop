# BIG CRUMB: Art Direction (v3: "Block Backyard")

> **Base: Pokopia** (*Pokémon Pokopia*). Reviewers describe it as:
> - "blocky, modular terrain" with "a pastel colour palette";
> - "everything is built on a grid";
> - a "colourful, expressive" style;
> - "Minecraft × Animal Crossing" coziness.
>
> We adapt that for a game about being tiny. The backyard is a giant, cozy, blocky diorama, and our ants are polished little creatures living in it.

## What we take from Pokopia, and how it changes for ants

| Pokopia | BIG CRUMB |
|---|---|
| Grid-built blocky terrain | The yard is built from **soft rounded blocks**: grass-topped dirt cubes, stepped hills, a stepped-pyramid anthill. Because blocks are *huge* relative to an ant, one block is a ledge, two is a climb, and a gap between patio blocks is a canyon. **The block grid is the level design.** |
| Pastel, dreamy palette | Mint grass, butter light, peach sky, lilac shadows. Food is the most saturated thing in frame. |
| Cute, expressive creatures | The ants keep real anatomy (three segments, six legs, elbowed antennae) with a **clean glossy finish, big shiny eyes and expressive antennae**. Players are marked by a paint dab plus a scarf. |
| Cozy life-sim UI | Rounded paper-label stickers in *Fredoka*, with soft shadows and a slight tilt. |

## Rules

- **Blocks everywhere in nature:** terrain, grass tufts (tall blocky stalks), flowers (cube petals), clouds (block clusters), trees (cube canopies).
- **Human things stay smooth and real:**
  - the hose, the house, the kitchen;
  - the food: donut, crumbs, sugar cubes.
  - The contrast says "this was made by giants."
- **The Mom:** soft rounded shapes, chunky friendly proportions, everyday clothes (cardigan, sweatpants, fuzzy slippers), big readable comedic faces. Framed from the front or three-quarter: slippers, the swat, the scream.
- **Light:** bright, soft, dreamy. A warm sun, cool lilac shadows, a bit of bloom on highlights. Sunset turns peach to coral.
- **Camera:** third-person, low (1–3 ant-lengths behind). A shallow depth of field keeps the "tiny" feeling. Wide shots go up, never top-down satellite view.
- **Smooth animation (on ones),** with snappy anticipation and squash, and antennae always overlapping.
