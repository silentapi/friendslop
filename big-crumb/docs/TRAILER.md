# BIG CRUMB: Trailer Plan (v4)

## Research: rules we're holding ourselves to

Sources: Derek Lieu's trailer-editing blog (on hooks, capture, pacing, common mistakes, feelings before information, and over-editing), Simon Carless (GameDiscoverCo) on getting to the hook quickly, the presskit.gg editing guide, and coverage of the Big Walk and PEAK launches.

1. **Hook in the first shot, and no later than 10–15 s.** Spend one or two shots establishing the genre, then show what's different. Our hook is *four friends who are ants, carrying a snack 50× their size across a giant world.* So the first image is a giant donut walking across a counter on ant legs.
2. **Feelings before information.** Music leads. Friends' voices make it feel like a night with friends, not a feature list.
3. **One idea per shot.** Strip everything that doesn't serve it, including HUD. The only HUD shown is HUD that *is* the idea: grab dots on the grab shot, the grip ring on the climb.
4. **Composition and blocking are designed per shot.** Balance the frame, keep the subject readable (the hero ant at least about 8% of frame height, the action in the centre third), and plan how motion cuts into the next shot.
5. **Eye trace.** Each shot's focal point sits where the viewer's eye already is from the previous shot. Most shots resolve to centre-frame; exceptions are listed below.
6. **Threes, then stop.** Never more than three similar beats, and the third one should twist.
7. **Sections of about 5–16 s that escalate to a climax, then a rest, then the payoff.** Vary wide and close shots. Shots shorten toward the climax.
8. **Cards must be specific and evocative, never salesy.** The trailer should still work with them removed. We keep two, and both advance the story.
9. **Game audio matters.** Every action gets its sound (thump, splash, gulp), with friends' voices on top of the music.
10. **Don't over-edit.** Choose clarity over flash. The Big Walk announce trailer lingers on its world and lets the friends be the show, and PEAK's marketing ran on its funniest failures.

### Cut after review

- **The door crack.** After three passes it still didn't read on a phone ("no wasted shots"). The cut from the outdoor climb straight to the cabinet climb inside carries the threshold.
- **The floor-to-counter tilt in the kitchen.** From the floor, the prize was a speck. It's replaced by the counter-lip crest.

### Cut from v3, and why

- **The scent-trail "far" shot.** It's a feature shot, and shot 4's reveal already says "far".
- **The "hill" shot.** A second roll beat repeats the idea.
- **The separate "steps" shot.** It's folded into "freeze" so the tension builds in one held shot.
- **Three cards.** They only explained what the footage already shows.
